How to Schedule Box League Matches

A practical box league scheduling guide for club organisers, with a court-capacity check, a sample round and a plan for stalled fixtures.

Published 25 November 2026

Give players a fixture list, a deadline and one place to share availability. Then let each pair of opponents agree a time and book a court. The organiser’s job is to spot fixtures that have stalled and help where needed.

Check court capacity before setting the deadline

In a round where everyone plays everyone else once, a box of five players or pairs produces ten matches. Four boxes of that size produce 40 matches. If the club has eight suitable court slots a week, six weeks offers 48 slots on paper. If two slots a week go unused because opponents cannot both attend, only 36 matches fit. Check the times players can realistically use before committing to a round length.

Count each court slot once, even if several boxes could use it. Leave room for weather, rescheduling and school holidays. If most members can play only after work, adding more lunchtime slots may make little difference.

Give players a simple booking process

  1. Ask every player to add their availability in week one. For doubles, collect it from all four players.
  2. Ask one player per fixture to propose two times that suit everyone and check court availability.
  3. Have that player book through the club’s usual system and send the date, time and court number to the other players.
  4. If a fixture moves, update the agreed match time and release the old court booking under the club’s cancellation rules.

Review stalled fixtures halfway through

In a six-week round, check progress at the end of week three. Start with fixtures that have no confirmed date. Ask anyone with missing availability to add it. When both opponents have responded but their calendars never overlap, check whether a weekend or quieter court slot would help. If a player has withdrawn, use the policy you shared at launch so the table is handled consistently.

Reminder to adapt: “Your match with Priya still needs a date. Please add your availability by Thursday, then agree a time and book your court. If none of your times overlap, let me know this week.”

Keep booking and results visible

Track which matches still need a date, which are booked and which have a recorded score. A booked match with no score after its scheduled date needs a different reminder from a match that was never arranged. Give players a final reminder one week before the deadline, including the procedure for reporting unplayed matches.
