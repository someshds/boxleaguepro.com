How to Run a Badminton Box League

Organise a badminton box league with a practical doubles format, a worked court booking plan and clear rules for scores and substitutes.

Published 27 November 2026

A badminton box league gives club members a small group of opponents and a deadline to play them. If your club already hires courts for a weekly session, start by reserving a few match slots within that booking and leave the other courts available for social play.

Choose singles or fixed pairs

Run separate singles and doubles boxes if you have enough entries. In doubles, enter as a pair and keep that pairing for the round. If members usually rotate partners at club night, let them find a partner during a warm-up session before entries close. Group players by recent results and your coaches’ or captains’ knowledge so newcomers start at a useful level.

Work out how many courts you need

Four pairs in one box create six fixtures. With three boxes, you need 18 match slots. If you reserve two courts for two match slots each on five club nights, you have room for 20 matches. That leaves two slots for rearrangements. If that buffer feels tight, extend the round or set aside another evening. Check how long a match actually takes at your venue before dividing up the session.

Agree the match and shuttle arrangements

Tell players how many games to play, what happens when the booked slot ends, what shuttle type to use and who provides it. If a fixture uses the end of a social session, agree when players can warm up and how they hand the court back. Publish one score-entry deadline and name the person who can correct a disputed score.

Make a rule for substitutes before play starts

One simple policy is to keep league fixtures to registered pairs and let a substitute join only for a friendly. If you permit substitutes in league matches, tell players who is eligible, how often they can substitute and whether results count in full. Keep walkovers and unfinished matches separate from played scores.

Publish the next round promptly

Check outstanding fixtures halfway through the round. When results close, show the table and the proposed movements together, then ask pairs to confirm they’re returning. If a partnership changes, reassess its starting box based on the new pairing.
